# Research 01: pointer events for tab drag

Evidence: throwaway lab (one HTML page loaded by `file://`, three Playwright scripts), headless Chromium 158 as Playwright launches it, real `page.mouse`/`page.keyboard` input. Lab deleted afterwards. "Capture" means `setPointerCapture` on the pressed `<button class="ptab">` at a 4 px threshold, called from a `document` `pointermove` listener.

## Findings

| # | Question | Observed | Design consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `click` after a drag with capture | Click **always** fires, target = the captured tab, whatever the release point: other pane, sibling tab, body, outside the viewport. The document `[data-view]` handler shows the view every time. `releasePointerCapture` inside a capture-phase `pointerup` listener changes nothing: `pointerup` and `click` still target the tab (the target is fixed before dispatch). PE3: with capture, click goes to the capture target. | Every completed drag must suppress its click. |
| 1b | Same, no capture | Click goes to the common ancestor of the down and up targets (`#panes`, `header`, `BODY`, `HTML`). Release back over the same tab: click on the tab, view shown. | Suppression is needed with or without capture. |
| 2 | Click timing; how to suppress | Order in one task: `pointerup` → `mouseup` → `lostpointercapture` → `click` → (rAF) → `setTimeout(0)`. A capture-phase `window` click listener registered in `pointerup`, calling `stopImmediatePropagation()` + `preventDefault()` and removed by `setTimeout(0)`, ate the drag's click; the next real click on another tab worked. When no click followed, the timeout removed it and the next real click was unaffected. `preventDefault` on `pointerup` or `mouseup` does **not** stop `click`. | Use the one-shot window capture listener, armed in `pointerup`, cleared by `setTimeout(0)`. No stale-flag risk. |
| 2b | Keyboard clicks | Space or Enter on the focused tab during a drag synthesises a `click` on the tab (keyboard clicks have `detail == 0`, `pointerType ""`, `pointerId -1`; mouse clicks have `detail >= 1`, `pointerType "mouse"`). | The suppressor should eat only `detail > 0` clicks, so a key that cancels the drag and is "handled as usual" still activates. Note that Space/Enter after a key cancel activates the tab, which is arguably the usual handling. |
| 3a | Text selection | Starting on a `<button>`: **no** selection in any variant (capture or not, across text in either pane, no `selectstart`). The baseline drag starting in text selected 152 chars. | No selection work is needed for button tabs. `user-select:none` on the body during the drag is a cheap backstop; `removeAllRanges()` is unnecessary. |
| 3b | `preventDefault()` on `pointerdown` | Suppresses `mousedown`/`mouseup` (compat events); **keeps a focused `<input>` focused** (today the tab takes focus and the input blurs); `click` still fires; it also stops selection starting on text. With it, keys typed during the drag land in the input (`x` was inserted). | Don't call it: it changes today's focus behaviour and redirects keystrokes. |
| 4 | Capture and other listeners | With capture: 0 `pointermove`/`pointerover` reached the canvas's or other elements' own listeners; all moves target the tab and bubble to `#panes`, `document` and `window`. `:hover` stays on the **tab** (`#hov:hover` false under the pointer). Without capture, the canvas got 6 moves and enter/leave events. Hit-testing for the drop must use `elementFromPoint`/rects, not `e.target`. | Capture isolates Graph and sash handlers from moves. The pane-under-pointer can't be read from CSS `:hover`. |
| 5 | Captured tab removed mid-drag (`tab.remove()` or `#panes.innerHTML` rebuild) | `focusout` fires synchronously. `lostpointercapture` fires **asynchronously** (after a microtask and a `setTimeout(0)`, before the next rAF) with target **`document`**: it never reaches the old tab or `#panes`. Subsequent moves hit-test normally (they reach the canvas and the text). `pointerup` goes to the element under the pointer, and **no `click` fires**. Calling `#panes.setPointerCapture(id)` right after removal works: `gotpointercapture@panes`, and `pointerup` targets `#panes`. | Listen for `lostpointercapture` on `document`. Either cancel the drag on it, or re-capture on the surviving host. Capturing on `#panes` from the start (test C: click target becomes `#panes`, so the `[data-view]` handler ignores it) survives skeleton rebuilds but still produces a host-level click. |
| 6 | Window blur | A synthetic `window` blur reaches only `window` listeners; capture is kept and the drag continues. Real focus loss can't be produced headless: after a second page is brought to front, `document.hasFocus()` stays true, with no blur and no events (Playwright emulates focus per page). `pointercancel` never fired for the mouse in any blur, key or removal case. It **did** fire (target = element under pointer) when the tab was `draggable=true` and native DnD started: PE3 fires `pointercancel` when the UA starts a drag-and-drop operation. | Handle `window` `blur` yourself (cancel and release capture); test it with `dispatchEvent(new Event('blur'))`. Keep tabs non-draggable, with no `<a>`/`<img>` inside, or native DnD will cancel the stream. |
| 7 | Keys while the button is held | `keydown` Escape/Shift/`a` reach `document` and `window` normally, target = the focused element (the tab, since pressing focuses it). Capture survives, and Chromium does not cancel anything on Esc. | Esc handling is entirely ours: a capture-phase `keydown` on `window` (or `document`) during a drag. Ignore `Shift`/`Control`/`Alt`/`Meta` keys. |
| 8 | Playwright input | `mouse.move(x,y,{steps:10})` gives exactly 10 `pointermove`s with fractional interpolated coords, the last exact. `mouse.down()` without a move uses the last position (`41,41`, `pointerId 1`, `mouse`). About 16 ms per step (frame-paced): steps 1, 10 and 30 take 12, 166 and 501 ms. A rAF scheduled in each `pointermove` had **already run** when `mouse.move` resolved (10/10). | Use 5-10 steps. No extra waits are needed for rAF-driven overlays, though auto-retrying `expect` remains the safe assertion style. |
| 9 | `touch-action` (cited, not tested) | PE3 "Declaring candidate regions for direct manipulation": with the default `touch-action:auto` the UA may pan/zoom on a touch drag and then fires `pointercancel` ("suppressing a pointer event stream"). Touch also gets implicit capture on `pointerdown`. | Put `touch-action:none` on `.ptab` if touch drags matter. |
| 10 | Fractional coords | `PointerEvent.clientX` = `100.37` (also `offsetX`, `pageX`, `screenX`); `MouseEvent.clientX` on `mousemove` = `100` (integer). | The `Reflect` read of `clientX` as f64 is correct. web-sys `client_x()` is `i32` and loses the fraction. |

## Recommended handler design

**State:** `Idle | Pending{pid, tab, view, x0, y0} | Dragging{pid, tab, view, overlay} | Cancelled{pid}`. `Cancelled` exists only to swallow the rest of the press.

- **`pointerdown` on `#panes`** (existing listener; the focus logic runs as today). If `button == 0`, `isPrimary`, the target's closest is `.ptab`, and the state is Idle, go to **Pending**. Don't `preventDefault`, so the tab still takes DOM focus and blurs inputs as today.
- **`pointermove`** (on `document`; with capture the events bubble there from the tab). For **Pending**, once the distance from `x0`/`y0` reaches the threshold, call `tab.setPointerCapture(pid)`, set `body.style.userSelect='none'` (optional backstop), show the overlay and go to **Dragging**. For **Dragging**, hit-test with `elementFromPoint(clientX, clientY)` or pane rects (not `e.target` or `:hover`) and update the overlay synchronously or in rAF. Read coords as f64.
- **`pointerup`** (capture-phase on `window`, matching `pid`):
  - **Pending:** go to Idle and let the native click do its usual work. Under the threshold that is today's click.
  - **Dragging:** compute the drop, arm the click suppressor, apply the dock, then go to Idle. Let the implicit release free the capture, so no `releasePointerCapture` is needed (calling it changes nothing).
  - **Cancelled:** arm the suppressor and go to Idle.
- **Click suppressor:** add a one-shot `window` capture listener for `click`. If `e.detail > 0`, call `stopImmediatePropagation()` and `preventDefault()`. Remove it on first use or by `setTimeout(0)`, whichever comes first, since the click arrives in the same task as `pointerup`.
- **Cancel paths** (`keydown` with `Dragging`/`Pending`; `window` `blur`; `pointercancel`; `lostpointercapture` on **`document`** while Dragging):
  - Hide the overlay, restore `user-select`, and `releasePointerCapture` if it is still held.
  - Go to **Cancelled** if the button may still be down (key, blur, lost capture); go to Idle on `pointercancel`, which has no click.
  - For Esc: `preventDefault()` + `stopPropagation()` (do nothing else). Pass other keys through. Ignore modifier-only keys.
  - A `lostpointercapture` that arrives after our own pointerup-driven transition (state already Idle) is a no-op.
  - If the arrangement rebuild is ours (a dock), it happens after Idle, so its async document-targeted `lostpointercapture` must not cancel anything.
- **Guard:** only one gesture at a time (ignore a second `pointerId`). Never set `draggable`, and keep `<a>`/`<img>` out of tabs. Consider `touch-action:none` on `.ptab`.

**Test notes:** drive with `mouse.move`, `down`, `move({steps:5..10})`, `up`. Simulate blur by dispatching it. Assert on the absence of view change after drag, and on presence after a 2 px press-release. Check Esc mid-drag leaves the layout untouched and that the release afterwards shows no view.
